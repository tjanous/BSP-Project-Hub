How this tracker works

What this is

A factual, non-partisan reference on where likely 2028 presidential candidates stand on energy and transportation policy, built for professionals who work in those industries. Positions are recorded without evaluation: nothing here is an endorsement, a score, or a criticism. An entry appears only when it is specific enough to distinguish candidates from one another; generic talking points that every candidate shares are excluded on purpose.

Where entries come from

An automated research scan runs every two weeks across public records: congressional votes, executive actions, signed and sponsored legislation, campaign statements, speeches, and press coverage. Each finding is extracted under strict rules that record what happened, when it happened, and what kind of evidence supports it, with concrete actions weighted above statements.

The sourcing standard

Every position cites its sources, and every source link is automatically verified to resolve before publication. If a working source can't back an entry, the entry is not published. Summaries state only what the sources document, never inferences about motives or intent.

What a “position shift” means

A shift flag marks a documented reversal only: the same yes-or-no policy question answered on opposite sides at two points in time, each side backed by an explicit statement, vote, veto, or signature. Pursuing the same goal more aggressively, adding detail to an existing plan, changing framing or emphasis, or going quiet on an issue is never flagged. Every candidate flag must pass multiple independent automated reviews unanimously, plus structural checks. When the evidence is uncertain, the tracker's rule is no flag. The filter is re-tested against a bank of real past examples before any change to it goes live.

Spot something wrong or outdated? Report it. A person reviews every submission, and corrections cite the source you point us to.
